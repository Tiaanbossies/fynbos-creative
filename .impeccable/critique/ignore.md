## `broken-image` — 4 findings in `BrandMark`

**Files:** `src/components/BrandMark.css:3`, `:30`, `:68`; `src/components/BrandMark.jsx:16`

Three of the four are the literal string `<img>` appearing inside explanatory comments, where the
comments describe how `BrandMark` renders — the detector cannot tell prose from markup.

The fourth, `BrandMark.jsx:16`, is a real `<img>` whose `src={SOURCE[variant]}` is resolved from a
lookup at runtime. The detector reads the attribute statically, sees no literal path, and reports
it missing. The asset exists and is generated by `scripts/build-logo-assets.mjs`.

## `overused-font` and `design-system-font` — 2 findings, one line

**File:** `src/components/SeoPanel.css:46` — `font-family: Arial`

Deliberate. This sets the fake Google wordmark inside the `aria-hidden` search-result illustration
on the homepage SEO panel. It is a depiction of someone else's brand, not this site's typography,
and it would be wrong in Zilla Slab or Work Sans. It must not be added to `DESIGN.md`'s type stack,
which is what would silence the `design-system-font` rule.

## `design-system-color` — 4 findings in the same illustration

**File:** `src/components/SeoPanel.css:53`, `:56`, `:59`, `:62` — `#4285f4`, `#ea4335`, `#fbbc05`, `#34a853`

Google's own brand hexes, drawing the four-colour "G" in the same `aria-hidden` illustration. Same
reasoning as the Arial above: these are a depiction, and **must never become tokens**. Adding them

`Pricing.css` records the related decision — the last hard-coded colour standing in for a *brand*
value was replaced with `--paper`; these literals stayed precisely because they are not brand values.

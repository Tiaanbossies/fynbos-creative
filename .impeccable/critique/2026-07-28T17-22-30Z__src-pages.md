**Cognitive load: moderate (2 of 8 checklist items fail).** Visual hierarchy fails on the interior pages — Services, Pricing and FAQ are a single undifferentiated column of hairline-divided rows with no secondary rhythm. Top-level choices sit at 6 (five nav items plus the CTA), one over the working-memory comfort line. Everything else passes: three pricing tiers, three form fields, one obvious action per screen.

**Emotional journey.** The peak is well placed — the founder's face and name appear above the fold on the homepage, and the case study lands immediately after, so trust arrives before any price. The valley is the interior pages, which drop from the composed homepage into what looks like an unstyled document. The end is strong on every page (a warm, honest closing CTA), except the 404, which ends on "this page is built in a later phase."

## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does this look AI-generated? Mostly no — and that is a real achievement.** The palette is a committed olive/terracotta/parchment system with a documented contrast contract, not the cream-and-slate default. The type is Zilla Slab + Work Sans, neither on the reflex-reject list. There are no gradient headings, no glassmorphism, no hero-metric template, no tracked-uppercase eyebrow above every section, no side-stripe borders. Copy is specific and refuses to invent proof. A visitor would not guess an AI made this.

Two places drift toward the reflex:

- **The Values row** is the textbook shape — three equal columns, small icon above heading above 14px muted body, centred text. It is the only centred block on the site, and the centring is what makes it read as a template rather than as part of this page.
- **The interior pages have no imagery at all.** Services, Pricing and FAQ are pure text. The brand register's own rule is that text-only pages where typography carries the entire visual weight are the failure mode, and here the typography is not carrying it — it is just left-aligned in a narrow column with half the screen blank.

**Deterministic scan** (`detect.mjs` over `src/`, exit 2, 5 findings): all five are false positives, worth recording as such so a future run does not re-litigate them.

- 4 × `broken-image` — three are the literal string `<img>` inside explanatory comments in `BrandMark.css` and `BrandMark.jsx`; the fourth is `BrandMark.jsx:37`, a real `<img>` whose `src={SOURCE[variant]}` the regex cannot resolve.
- 1 × `overused-font` — `font-family: Arial` at `SeoPanel.css:46` is the deliberately fake Google wordmark inside the `aria-hidden` search-result illustration. Correct as written.

**Browser overlay**: injected successfully into the live page; overlays are visible in the browser tab. The in-page detector found **1** anti-pattern — `cramped-padding`, an anchor with 0px vertical padding. Traced to the two inline links in the contact block (`tiaan@fynboscreative.co.za` and `privacy policy`), which paint 16px tall against the 24×24 minimum WCAG 2.5.8 asks for. Notably, `HomeFaq.css:58` already documents fixing exactly this class of bug for the "See all questions" link — the same fix was never applied to these two.

**Contrast: clean.** I measured 19 rendered text/background pairs across the site. The lowest is 5.35:1 (the terracotta script kicker and the step numerals); body copy runs 11.36:1 and muted copy 5.47:1. The footer on olive measures 5.55:1. Nothing fails AA. The `--bark-grey` / `--bark-deep` split in `tokens.css` is doing its job.

## Overall Impression

This is an honest, well-argued site with a genuinely distinctive homepage and four interior pages that look like they were never designed. The homepage has composition — a split hero, a real screenshot, an offer row with a vertical hairline, an illustrated SEO panel. Open `/services` at 1440px and you get a left-aligned ~575px column of text in a 1440px window; the right 55% is empty paper. The same is true of `/pricing`, `/faq` and `/about`. The craft in the codebase is high and the reasoning in the comments is unusually good — the gap is not care, it is that the mockup only ever covered the homepage, and the interior pages inherited its restraint without inheriting its composition.

The single biggest opportunity: give the interior pages a second axis. Not cards — the no-card rule is right and should hold — but something that occupies the page: a full-width band behind one section, an asymmetric two-column intro, the founder photo or a delivered-work image on Services, sizes that vary between rows. Right now every row on every interior page has the same weight, which is Nielsen #8's "visual noise floor" inverted: nothing competes, so nothing leads.

## What's Working

1. **The failure path in the contact form is exemplary.** `contact.js` throws rather than resolving quietly when Formspree is unconfigured, and `Contact.jsx` turns that into "Sorry, that didn't send" plus WhatsApp *and* email, with the visitor's typing left in the fields. Field errors are plain, specific and actionable ("That doesn't look like a phone number or email address"). Most production sites get this wrong; this one reasoned about it and wrote the reasoning down.

2. **The sticky WhatsApp bar yields to in-page CTAs.** `StickyWhatsApp.jsx` observes *every* `[data-primary-cta]` and hides while any is 60% visible, so the hero never shows two identical terracotta pills a thumb apart — and it fails open, so the primary conversion path never depends on the observer working. That is a real design decision with a real fallback.

3. **Honesty is visibly load-bearing, not decorative.** The SEO panel refuses to draw a fake "#1 result". The pricing table has no "Most popular" badge. The case study exists only because consent was confirmed and carries no invented numbers. Prices appear on Services, Pricing, and the homepage — never behind a "contact us". This is the brand promise implemented as constraints in code, and it is the site's strongest asset.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The 404 page tells visitors the site is unfinished

`App.jsx:33-35` — every unmatched route renders `PagePlaceholder`, whose body reads **"Placeholder — this page is built in a later phase."** The component's own docstring says it "must not survive review". It survived. A visitor arriving from a stale link, a mistyped URL, or an old business card is told, on a site whose entire pitch is credibility, that the business has not finished building its own website. The body also contains no link, no CTA, and no explanation — only the header and footer offer a way onward.

**Why it matters:** this is the one page where a visitor is already slightly lost, and it converts confusion into doubt about the business. It is also the page most likely to be seen by someone who was actively trying to reach you.

**Fix:** split the 404 from the phase-placeholder. Give `*` a real page: "That page isn't here" + one line of plain reassurance + the WhatsApp CTA + links to Services, Pricing and FAQ. Keep `PagePlaceholder` for unbuilt nav routes only, or delete it — `BUILT_ROUTES` currently covers every entry in `NAV_LINKS`, so its only live use *is* the 404.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The four interior pages leave half the desktop viewport empty

`/services`, `/pricing`, `/about` and `/faq` all cap their content at 54–60ch inside a 72rem container and left-align it. At 1440×900 that is a ~575px column of text against ~700px of blank paper. `/pricing` compounds it with two very large empty vertical bands (between the build fee and "Then pick a monthly plan", and between the rescue block and the closing CTA) created by `padding-block-end: 0` on the intro stacking against the next section's full `--section-y`.

**Why it matters:** the measure cap is correct for readability and should stay. What is missing is anything else on the page. A visitor who arrives on `/pricing` from Google — the page the SEO work is aimed at — meets what looks like a draft, immediately after a homepage that looked designed. This is the single largest source of the aesthetic/minimalist score of 2.

**Fix:** give each interior page one compositional move that fills the width without adding cards. Candidates: a full-bleed olive or parchment band behind one section (the closing CTA is the obvious one, and it would echo the footer); an asymmetric intro with the heading in a wide left column and the lede offset right; the delivered Bossie's Gym screenshot on `/services`; the founder portrait promoted out of its 2fr column on `/about`. Then collapse the doubled vertical gaps on `/pricing`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] Homepage FAQ rows look like static text; the FAQ page's don't

`HomeFaq.css:25-37` removes the native disclosure triangle (`list-style: none` + `::-webkit-details-marker`) and never replaces it. `FaqPage.jsx:62-73` renders a rotating chevron for the identical pattern. On the homepage, "How much does a website cost?" and its three siblings render as four plain hairline-divided lines of olive text with no indication that anything happens when tapped.

**Why it matters:** these four questions were deliberately chosen as the last objections between a warm visitor and a message — cost, why monthly, lock-in, technical skill. If a visitor doesn't know they open, the section answers nothing and reads as a teaser list. The audience is explicitly non-technical and scanning on a phone; they will not tap a line of text to see what happens.

**Fix:** give `.home-faq-question` the same chevron `FaqPage` uses. The two files are namespaced apart on purpose, so copy the icon markup rather than merging the components — but the affordance must match.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P1] The privacy policy claims analytics that are not installed, and the one success metric is unmeasured

`PrivacyPolicy.jsx:31-37` states "We use Google Analytics to understand how visitors use this site". No analytics snippet exists anywhere — `index.html` loads no tag, and `whatsapp.js:20` guards `trackWhatsAppClick` behind `typeof window.gtag !== 'function'`, so every WhatsApp click call silently no-ops.

**Why it matters:** two separate problems from one gap. First, a POPIA page on a site selling honesty states something untrue about data collection — the exact failure mode the rest of the copy is disciplined about avoiding. Second, `PRODUCT.md` defines success as "a WhatsApp click", the code is already instrumented to count them by source (`hero`, `pricing`, `contact`, `sticky-mobile`, `contact-form-fallback`), and nothing is receiving those events. The business cannot tell which page or CTA is working.

**Fix:** pick one and make the page match. Either install the analytics the policy describes (or a lighter, cookieless alternative, rewording the paragraph to name it), or delete the Google Analytics paragraph. Do not leave the claim standing while the instrumentation is dark.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Four content patterns, four different treatments

The site states a clear rule — open rows and hairlines, no cards — then applies it four ways:

- Homepage pricing uses bordered, shadowed, hover-lifting **cards** (`Pricing.css`), while `/pricing` uses open columns with a sage wash on Bloom (`PricingTiers.css`). Same three tiers, two visual languages, one click apart.
- Homepage FAQ has no chevron; `/faq` has one (Issue 3).
- The Values row is centre-aligned; every other block on the site is left-aligned.
- `/faq` rows stretch the full 72rem container, so on desktop the question sits ~1000px from its own chevron, while every other page bounds its rows to ~60ch.

Additionally, `Values.css:44` makes `.values-mark` a **fourth grid column** at ≥60rem, so a 70px terracotta protea glyph floats to the right of the three values, vertically centred against nothing and squeezing the columns beside it. It reads as an orphaned element, not as punctuation.

**Why it matters:** the no-card rule is a real brand position (questionnaire §08) and the tokens file documents where it was knowingly reversed. But a visitor moving homepage → pricing sees the same information twice in two costumes, which quietly undermines the "one obvious next action" principle.

**Fix:** pick one tier treatment and use it in both places. Bound `/faq` rows to the same measure as the rest of the site. Left-align the Values row, and either integrate the protea mark into the section (a full stop after the last column, baseline-aligned) or drop it.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)** — the persona this site is actually built for, and it mostly holds up. The sticky WhatsApp bar is always in the thumb zone, video is skipped under Save-Data, no horizontal overflow at 390px, form state survives a failed send. What breaks: the footer nav links, the contact email link and the "privacy policy" link all paint 16–24px tall, under the 44px comfortable target and (for two of them) under the 24×24 WCAG minimum. The "Phone number or email" field is `type="text"`, so a phone gets a letter keyboard for a field that is usually a number — on a page whose whole job is making contact cheap.

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer)** — well served on copy, badly served on one affordance. Every price is visible without asking, every heading is a plain sentence, no icon-only navigation, no jargon. But Jordan reads the homepage "Before you ask" section, sees four questions with no answers and no visible way to get them, and concludes the site is teasing. Jordan also can't tell that the homepage price cards and the `/pricing` tiers are the same three plans.

**Riley (Deliberate Stress Tester)** — finds the 404 in about ninety seconds and gets told the page is "built in a later phase". Submits the contact form with all three fields empty: gets three good, specific error messages, but focus stays on the submit button and nothing announces that validation failed, so a keyboard or screen-reader user has to hunt for what went wrong. Notices the privacy policy claims analytics that the network tab shows are never loaded.

**Marlize (project persona: non-technical SA business owner, 40s, on a phone, metered data, referred by a friend)** — arrives via WhatsApp link on `/pricing`. Sees an honest R1,200 and three plans, which is exactly what she came for and is genuinely reassuring. Then scrolls through two screens of empty paper between sections and is not sure whether the page finished loading. Taps "services page", lands on another single column of text. Never sees a photograph of anything after leaving the homepage. Her doubt is not about price — it's whether this is a real business with real work behind it, and the interior pages give her nothing to look at that answers it.

## Minor Observations

- `base.css:45-54` applies `font-weight: 700` to h1–h4 while `tokens.css:80-84` states headings "go back to 600". The comment already flags the contradiction; it is still unresolved. Pick one.
- No focus management on form submit — focus should move to the first invalid field, and the error count should reach `aria-live`.
- The hero's sage leaf and the SEO panel's olive leaf both render as soft blobs rather than reading as leaves at their painted size; at desktop the SEO one looks like a smudge behind the heading.
- `/pricing` heading "Then pick a monthly plan" depends on the reader having processed the build-fee block above it — fine on desktop, weaker after two screens of gap on mobile.
- `App.jsx` renders `PagePlaceholder` for `NAV_LINKS` entries outside `BUILT_ROUTES`, but every nav entry is currently built — so the placeholder's only live role is the 404. Worth simplifying once the 404 gets a real page.
- The contact form's honeypot, `noValidate` + custom validation, and permissive phone matching are all correct calls and well documented. No change wanted.

## Questions to Consider

- The homepage got a mockup and the interior pages didn't. What would `/services` look like if it had been designed with the same ambition — and does it need the Bossie's Gym screenshot more than the homepage does?
- The no-card rule is a real position, but the homepage already broke it for pricing on the mockup's authority. Is the rule "no cards", or is it "no *boxed* cards" — and which of the two do the interior pages actually need?
- If the one measure of success is a WhatsApp click, what is the cost of another month of not counting them?
- The 404 is the only page that speaks in the site's own internal voice ("a later phase"). Where else might build-time language be visible to a visitor?
